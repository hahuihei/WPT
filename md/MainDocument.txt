Abstract—This paper investigates an efﬁciency model predic- tive  control  (MPC)  strategy  aimed  at  ensuring  rapid  response and stable current regulation in wireless power transfer (WPT) system with considering hard constraints. The current ﬂuctuation caused by the load variation of WPT is an inherent problem, and the  goal of the MPC  controller is to maintain a constant current  (CC)  of  DC-DC  converter  on  the  receiver  side,  and achieve  fast  and  safe  charging  against  when  load  resistance changes. In order to achieve this goal, a tracking performance function that utilizes a simpliﬁed linear prediction model on DC- DC converter is ﬁrst considered, which allows a low computation burden. Furthermore, the constraint limitations on duty cycle, current and voltage are also incorporated for circuit safety so that the controller optimizes the duty cycle under the consideration of constraint handling. Finally, a receding horizon optimization problem  is  formulated  for  optimal  current  tracking,  and  the global optimal solution of duty cycle can be obtained from the resulting quadratic program (QP) problem. This approach is able to stabilize the output CC of WPT system with a rapid response under load resistance variation conditions, and also provides good steady-state  performance  and  dynamic  regulation  capabilities. The experimental results are provided to demonstrate the effec- tiveness of the designed MPC method for achieving satisfactory set-point tracking performance.
Index Terms—Model predictive control (MPC), current regula- tion, wireless power transfer (WPT), DC-DC converter, set-point tracking.

I.  INTRODUCTION
ITH the automotive industry development processing in transition to more efﬁcient and less polluting, the
growth rate of various electric vehicles is exponentially in- creasing. Battery charging has now become  a core issue in the  industry  circle.  Wireless power  transfer  (WPT)  system, which refers to a kind of energy transmission from the power supply to the load without direct electrical contact, is gaining an increasing attention in commercial market. WPT  system exhibits greater portability and ﬂexibility, eliminating the need for a direct connection between the power source and electrical devices. This allows electrical equipment to be moved more freely  without  being  constrained  by  cables,  enhancing  the stability of the connection. WPT system typically operates on the principles of elec- tromagnetic induction or magnetic resonance coupling. The basic components of a WPT system include a transmitter coil, a receiver coil, and associated power electronics for energy conversion and control. The transmitting coil generates a high- frequency alternating magnetic ﬁeld through high-frequency alternating current, and the receiving coil receives the magnetic ﬁeld and converts it into electrical energy, thus transferring the electrical energy to the load. Signiﬁcant research efforts have been directed toward addressing the challenges for WPT system  in  optimization  and  control.  These  efforts  can  be broadly  categorized  into  different  areas:  coil  and  topology designs, power electronics and control strategies. For example, a two-stage strategy for unmanned aerial vehicles WPT system that  considers  a  supplying  power  to  energy-limited  sensor nodes was investigated in  [6]. In  [7],  a reconﬁgurable half- bridge compensation topology for WPT system is studied to achieve  constant  current  and  voltage  outputs.  These  efforts mainly focus on optimizing coil geometry and conﬁgurations to enhance efﬁciency and power transfer capability. In practice, WPT  system  often  suffers  from  efﬁciency  degradation  due to  factors  such  as  coil  misalignment,   load  variation,  and environmental interference.
In response to above challenges, another signiﬁcant areas of progress  in  WPT  system  is  the  development  of  various control strategies to enhance efﬁciency and robustness from different perspectives. Jou et al. proposed a bidirectional DC- DC WPT system using inductor-capacitor-capacitor-capacitor (LCC-C) resonance compensation network in  [8], where the controller was designed by a PI controller. In [9],the controller was designed based on the measured active power and reactive power at the resonant network of bidirectional WPT system to regulate the power ﬂow in both directions. In order to improve the robustness of WPT system with coil misalignment, Xia et al. designed a μ-controller in [10] based on structured singular value to reduce transmission power ﬂuctuations. In fact, most works in the existing literature adopt classical PID controller in WPT system due to its ﬂexibility to regulate system output voltage, current, and power. However, the performance of a PID  controller  depends  heavily  on  choosing  proper  param- eters, which inevitably leads to a decrease in the controller



robustness. In order to overcome this drawback, some revelent papers have been reported to address this issue. For example, a adaptive frequency tracking control method was proposed for  WPT   system  in   [11]  to  ensure  the  resonant   state   of system based on the fuzzy PI controller. Liu et al. considered the  control  problem  of  the  rapid  variation  of  coil  mutual inductance in [12], where a PID controller was designed for DC-DC converter at the receive side to improve the perfor- mance  against  changing  coupling  coefﬁcient.  Despite  these improvements of PID method, the WPT  system in practice faces more challenges such as system nonlinearity and load variation. These challenges severely limit their applications to cope with more complicated control requirements. Therefore, in  order  to  solve  these  problems,  how  to  design  effective advanced control strategies such as sliding mode control [13], internal model control [14], and robust control [15] for WPT system have become a focal point of intense research to be continuously pursued.
In the existing advanced control method of WPT system, model predictive control (MPC), as a control algorithm orig- inated  in  industrial  circle,  has  been  recognized  a  potential approach  for  many  complex  mutivariable  systems  since  it can  precisely  regulate  the  system  based  on  a  mathematical model, thereby improving the system’s dynamic response. In the literature, it has been reported that MPC method on WPT system is superior to traditional methods since it is able to provide key beneﬁts such as good robustness, multi-objective optimization, rapid convergence in ﬂuctuating environments, and  less  ripple  in  steady-state  operation.  MPC  operates  by predicting future state trajectories in the discrete domain and computing an optimal control action for the immediate time step,  which  is  subsequently  implemented  by  the  controller. Many  scholars have investigated  various MPC  algorithm to WPT system and reported good results, see the reference and therein [16]–[18]. The existing MPC methods for WPT system can  be  divided  into  three  categories:  the  transmitting  side control [19], the receiving side control [20], [21], and the dual- side control [22]. In [19], a novel frequency selection MPC al- gorithm was proposed to determine the optimal initial switch- ing frequency of the transmitting side inverter. The optimal initial frequency generally ensures the zero-voltage switching (ZVS)  operation  of the  inverter.  However,  the  algorithm  is susceptible to notching effects and current distortion induced by dead time. Additionally, it fails to maintain soft switching in WPT system under light-load condition. Chen et al.  [22] designed  a  control   strategy  for  the  dynamic  improvement and  efﬁciency  optimization  of  dual-side  inductor-capacitor- inductor (LCL) compensated WPT system, where the inverter at the transmitting side was controlled by the perturbation and observation method for maximum energy efﬁciency tracking and the rectiﬁer at the receiver-side was  stabilized  a  ﬁnite- control-set  MPC  to  achieve  a  fast  output  voltage  response. The authors in [23] introduced an offset-free composite MPC strategy for the receiver-side buck converter, incorporating a sliding mode observer to estimate future tracking errors and address model uncertainties. It can be seen that MPC has been proved an effective method for WPT system and generated a good basis in these literature, however the designed controllers


ignore the physical constraints in the  optimization problem such as duty cycle, current, and voltage, which is adverse to its applications in practice. This motivates us to consider a constrained MPC controller for WPT system.
During  the  actual  operation  of  the  WPT  system,  the system  parameters  may  also  ﬂuctuate  due  to  the  model- ing  mismatch,  external  disturbance,  load  mutation,  power supply  voltage  ﬂuctuation  and  coupling  coefﬁcient  change. For  the  objective  of  achieving  the  desired  reference  track- ing,   exact   output   regulation   is   the   crucial   performance for   the   controller   in   WPT   system.   The   typical   meth- ods   consists   of   considering   different   compensation   net- works such as inductor-capacitor-capacitor-inductor-capacitor- capacitor (LCC-LCC) and inductor-capacitor-capacitor-series (LCC-S)  topology,  and  adding  a  DC-DC  converter  on  the transmitting  and  receiving  sides.  For  instance,  in  order  to achieve  constant  current  (CC)  charging,  a  reconﬁgurable topology that combines LCC-LCC and LCC-S compensation networks was designed in [24], but it relays on a bulky circuit design implementation. Meanwhile, the topology parameters severely constrain the design ﬂexibility of the charge current. Once the circuit parameters  are  ﬁxed, the  output current is vulnerable  for  the  variations  of  mutual  inductance  of  the coils or load resistance. These facts requires that the designed controller should have a rapid ability to regulate the dynamic system against ﬂuctuation of output current, thus the current regulation is the another concern of our article. Considering the fast response feature of MPC, a few works on DC-DC converter have been conducted in order to reduce the adverse effect of the rapid ﬂuctuations on the receiver-side of WPT system [21], [25]–[29]. In [26], the authors investigated the im- plementation problem of long-horizon ﬁnite-control-set MPC on an FPGA. Zhou et al. [21] considered the communication delay problem and designed a time-delay compensation MPC strategy  for  DC-DC  converter.  On  the  other  hand,  in  the literature, some MPC methods have been extensively studied regarding the optimal control issue for DC-DC converter. Mao et  al.  [30]  investigated  a  digital  average  current  MPC  with load  current  compensation  for  multiphase  interleaved  DC- DC converter to improve the load transient perforamnce. In [27],  a  robust  MPC  strategy  for  DC-DC  converter  in  DC microgrids was investigated to ensure the stable charging of constant  power  load.  In  these  work,  the  computation  time will grow exponentially with the length of the prediction and control horizons. In order to reduce the computation burden, a variable  self-tuning horizon  mechanism for MPC method of DC-DC  converter  was  studied  in  [31].  For  the  sake  of eliminating steady-state error, an offset-free composite MPC strategy  for  DC-DC  buck  converter  was  proposed  in  [32]. However, these two approaches are only effective for constant power loads. Recently in  [33], a noise tolerance method for DC-DC converters based on continuous control set MPC was designed.  In  these  works,  the  controllers  are  vulnerable to current distortion being caused by a load variation, this limits the application of the designed MPC methods. Moreover, the physical constraints of WPT system are ignored so that the controllers are incapable of handling the physical operation constraints. However, one of the vital features of MPC is its



ability to handling various system constraints in solving the optimization problem. Therefore, an efﬁcient MPC controller that  takes  the  circuit  constraint  condition  for  WPT  system remains an unexplored and practical topic, this further motivate us to investigate an MPC controller with a low computation burden that considers the constraint condition of WPT system, enabling the real-time implementation efﬁciency.
In this paper, in order to address the aforementioned moti- vation, our main goal is to investigate a constrained MPC for current regulation of WPT system, which targets to provide fast response and stable output current by tracking the desired reference  value   for  a  load  variation   condition.  The  main novelties of this paper are stated as follows.
1)  a linear discrete-time model of DC-DC converter on the receiver-side in the WPT system that utilizes state-space current averaging method is ﬁrst established for controller design.  Based  on  this  model,  the  implementation  of maintaining the constant current by controlling the DC- DC converter rather than the inverter is studied so that the power regulation for WPT system is more ﬂexible from the control view;
2)  a tracking quadratic performance function is designed by minimizing the tracking error of the load current and duty cycle. The optimal control input sequence of duty cycle can be obtained by minimizing the cost function based on the future system state and output predictions. Mean- while,  constraint  limitations  on  the  output  current  and duty cycle are imposed in the optimal control problem formulation so that they are inherently satisﬁed at each implementation of MPC controller to ensure the circuit safety operation;
3)  an  efﬁciency receding horizon optimization problem is formulated for achieve optimal current tracking of DC- DC converter, which is helpful to reduce the controller computational  burden.   The  optimal  control  input  se- quence of duty cycle is directly obtained from the solu- tion of a quadratic program (QP) problem, which could ensure optimal system transient performance such as fast response time and less oscillations.
The rest organization of this paper is addressed as follows. Section II shows the working principle, compensation topol- ogy, and control model of DC-DC converter for WPT system. Section III gives the designed MPC strategy. In Section IV, the validity of the designed MPC controller is demonstrated by experimental tests. Section V makes a conclusion for this paper.

C.  Dynamic Control and Quadratic Optimization Problem

The principle of MPC is to optimize the sequence of manip- ulated variable d(k) computed for time indices k to k+Nc , and only the ﬁrst sample of this sequence is applied as the current control signal and repeated at the next sampling instant. For the compensation network, the output voltage is affected by load change, this is unfavorable to maintain a high working efﬁciency  to  complete  the  charging  process.  Therefore,  the objective of the DC-DC converter is to accurately track its current  reference  as  well  as  maintaining  optimal  transient performance, even under signiﬁcant load variation.

More details regarding the conﬁguration and exper- imental results are shown in the following ﬁve subsections.


A.  Veriﬁcation Platform Setup
In Fig. 4, the experimental prototype for verifying the pro- posed MPC in WPT system is shown. The platform contains: 1) the full-bridge rectiﬁer; 2) transmitter-side compensation network; 3) transmitting and receiving coils; 4) receiver-side compensation  network;  5)  oscilloscope;  6)  data  acquisition (DAQ) card; 7) DC-DC converter; 8) current probe; 9) pro- grammable electronic load; 10) laptop; 11) DC source. In this prototype, the inverter working frequency  fT   and  the  buck converter’s switching frequency are both set to 100 kHz. The selection of the  size of the transmitting and receiving coils is  20x20cm2 .  The  main  parameter  conﬁguration  of  WPT system  in  this  paper  is  shown  in  Table   1.  The  controlled variables of voltage and current are obtained from DAQ card and  transmitted  to  the  laptop  to  determine  the  steady-state tracking setting points and optimal control vectors based on the MPC algorithm, where the MPC controller is coded utilizing Matlab 2020a m.ﬁles encapsulated in a S-function module and performed in a laptop with a Intel(R) Core(TM) Utral 5 125H CPU at 1.20GHZ and 32GB RAM.

V.  CONCLUSION
In this paper, a constrained MPC to achieve current regula- tion of DC-DC converter for WPT system is studied. Based on the equivalent circuit model of DC-DC converter, a linearized prediction model is given, then a tracking performance func- tion to current and duty cycle is designed. Meanwhile, for the sake of protecting circuit safety, the limitations on current and voltage are imposed in the controller on-line optimization at each sampling time. The optimal control sequence is obtained by  solving  a receding  horizon problem, which is described by  a  QP  problem,  so  that  the  controller  avoids  to  have  a high computation burden. The designed MPC controller has optimized  dynamics  and  accurate  current  set-point  tracking for mutual inductance and load variation in both dynamic per- formance and steady-state performance. Experimental results under various operating conditions validate both the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed control method.
