Abstract: This paper investigates an efficiency model predictive control (MPC) strategy aimed at
ensuring rapid response and stable current regulation in wireless power transfer (WPT)
system with considering hard constraints. The current fluctuation caused by the load
variation of WPT is an inherent problem, and the goal of the MPC controller is to
maintain a constant current (CC) of DC-DC converter on the receiver side, and achieve
fast and safe charging against when load resistance changes. In order to achieve this
goal, a tracking performance function that utilizes a simplified linear prediction model
on DC-DC converter is first considered, which allows a low computation burden.
Furthermore, the constraint limitations on duty cycle, current and voltage are also
incorporated for circuit safety so that the controller optimizes the duty cycle under the
consideration of constraint handling. Finally, a receding horizon optimization problem is
formulated for optimal current tracking, and the global optimal solution of duty cycle
can be obtained from the resulting quadratic program (QP) problem. This approach is
able to stabilize the output CC of WPT system with a rapid response under load
resistance variation conditions, and also provides good steady-state performance and
dynamic regulation capabilities. The experimental results are provided to demonstrate
the effectiveness of the designed MPC method for achieving satisfactory set-point
tracking performance.

Constrained Model Predictive Control for Current Regulation of DC-DC Converter for Wireless
PowerTransferSystem
• A simplified prediction model using the state-space current averaging method for wireless power transfer system is
established,enablingflexiblepowerregulationandlowercomputationalburdencomparedtoinverter-sidecontrol.
• A tracking performance function of model predictive control is designed that explicitly considers hard physical
constraintsondutycycle,outputcurrent,andvoltage,ensuringcircuitsafetyduringdynamictransitions.
• By formulating and solving a quadratic programming (QP) problem within a receding horizon, the controller in the
experiments achieves rapid set-point tracking with minimal oscillations and high steady-state accuracy despite load
resistancechanges.
